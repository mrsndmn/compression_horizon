Progressive Cramming: Reliable Token Compressionand What It Reveals

A frozen LM can compress >1000 tokens into one embedding — but perfect reconstruction is brittle steering, not transferable semantics.

1 Progressive Cramming

Optimize per-sample embedding e so a frozen LM reconstructs tokens. Extend target one token at a time, advance only after perfect convergence:

  • Halt on first failure → sharp capacity boundary
  • 100% greedy generation + honest per-sample capacity
  • Saved e at each step → optimizer trajectory

5 Attention Knockout
Llama-3.1-8B. Forward (mask early layers) restores downstream accuracy. Reverse fails until early layers are reached.

Early layers cause both reconstruction and capability collapse. Late-layer attention mass (20–60%) is a symptom. Consistent across Llama-3.1-8B, Pythia-1.4B, SmolLM2-1.7B.

6 Why Not Full Cramming?

Full cramming (Kuratov et al., 2025) fixes the number of tokens and optimizes all at once; progressive cramming fixes reconstruction accuracy at 100% and grows the sequence. Full cramming reaches higher capacity — but residual errors concentrate on early positions, and one early miss cascades through autoregressive generation:

7 Limitations & Future Work
  • Reconstruction ≠ semantics — we do not yet know how to make a single embedding both perfectly reconstructable and semantically faithful
  • Optimizer headroom — low-dim projection results suggest better optimizers could compress longer sequences than we reach
✓ Takeaways
  • Brittle Steering — perfect reconstruction does not preserve useful semantics in the tested tasks; downstream benchmarks drop, generative MMLU collapses to ~0%
  • Early Layers — across the three tested families, the embedding’s causal influence concentrates in the first few layers; late-layer attention mass is a symptom
